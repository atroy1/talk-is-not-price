## 1. What this is, in one paragraph

A pre-registered study for Apart Research's AI Collusion Research Sprint (Track 2, Detection and Audit; question 2.2,
"does combining what agents say with what they do beat either alone"). Five LLM sellers in a double-auction market
(Agrawal et al. 2025's starter and 75 prior markets) can message each other; we built a chat monitor and a price
screen, certified at k/(n+1) false-alarm rates on honest markets, and ran 160 new markets across seven model families.
Findings: no model family talks honestly when it can; the one sentence that produces honest talk ("coordinating prices
is illegal") makes Haiku honest, silences Sonnet, Opus and Luna, is ignored by DeepSeek and Qwen, and makes Sonnet
honest once it must send a note each round; calibrated on 20 honest Haiku markets (4.8%), the chat monitor flags every
talking cartel (35/35, 29/30, 10/10, 5/5, 5/5, 8/8), with no false alarm on 56 honest markets and identical results
under a second labeller family; what it flags is talk, not harm (16 of 35 cartels left buyers no worse off; the honest
no-chat market that sets the price screen's bar cost buyers more than any cartel); and a flag says nothing about silence
(one high silent market in six) or about strategy that outlived the 2025 overseer's gag (under CEO pressure the
sellers' private notebooks kept the plan and the price held). Working title: "Talk Is Not Price: What a Chat Monitor
Sees and Misses in LLM Seller Markets".
